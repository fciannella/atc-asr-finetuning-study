# Public community ATC ASR models: assessment and evaluation plan

Assessment date: **2026-11-06**. Status: **research and artifact screening completed; first evaluation group completed on all 1,908 test utterances**.

The strongest comparison is a mixture of ATCO2/Jacktol-adapted models and models adapted on separate simulator, synthetic, or US radio sources. Eight new checkpoints and the existing qenneth/P4/N4 baselines are now measured in the [completed benchmark](community-atc-benchmark-2026-11-06.md); remaining families are unmeasured. Do not rank models using WER values copied from unrelated model cards.

## Scope and coverage

This assessment concerns speech-to-text models adapted to air traffic control, with English ATCO2 as the target. It includes non-English/mixed-language ATC models as optional transfer experiments, audio-language models that transcribe speech, and deployment conversions. Text parsers, role classifiers, TTS, trajectory predictors, and generic aviation chatbots are outside the ASR comparison.

Hugging Face searches used `atc`, `atco`, `air-traffic`, `air_traffic`, `airtraffic`, `aviation`, `uwb`, `rasr`, `radio-asr`, and `radio-whisper`, plus `air-traffic-control`, `atc`, `atco2`, and `atcosim` tags. Pagination was exhausted rather than stopping at the first 150 results. Web searches checked research projects, possible non-Hub checkpoints, and streaming candidates.

- **574 unique public repository search hits** before relevance filtering.
- **287 repository records screened** through metadata, available cards, and artifact listings, including 27 extra ambiguous candidates checked beyond the initial speech filter.
- **38 repositories assessed in detail** below. ASTRA contains three separate ASR models, so repository and checkpoint counts differ.
- **27 conversion/adapter/decoder variants**, **81 additional candidates**, **62 with inadequate provenance documentation**, **39 without recognizable weights**, **14 with an unverified speech task**, **22 excluded hits**, and **4 adjacent radio controls**.

This is a broad, reproducible public inventory, not a guarantee that every existing ATC checkpoint has been found. Private models, poorly named repositories, and unindexed releases may be missing. “Weights listed” means an artifact appears in the repository tree; it does not mean we downloaded it, validated all shards, restored the model, or established rights to deploy it.

The [complete Markdown ledger](community-atc-model-inventory.md), [CSV inventory](../reports/community-atc-model-inventory-2026-11-06.csv), and [JSON evidence snapshot](../reports/community-atc-model-inventory-2026-11-06.json) retain repository IDs, exact revisions, author metadata, artifact paths, screening decisions, and sources. Raw third-party model-card prose is not copied into the report.

All additional repositories are retained in the [ledger](community-atc-model-inventory.md), including the large jlvdoorn size grid, luigisaetta variants, youngsangroh, allenpoe, AshtonLKY, SAadettin-BERber, samuelashraff, and other small/medium Whisper experiments. Confirmed or apparent deployment variants include CTranslate2, MLX, CoreML, ONNX, GGML/GGUF, WhisperKit, and adapter/merged pairs. Examples are [qenneth's GGUF conversion](https://huggingface.co/pronoobie/Parakeet-v3-For_ATC), [the jlvdoorn MLX conversion](https://huggingface.co/sfabriece/whisper-large-v3-atco2-asr-mlx), and [the chrullis LoRA form](https://huggingface.co/chrullis/qwen3-asr-radio-1.7b-lora). Evaluate one original trained checkpoint first; measure conversions separately for deployment parity and quantization loss. A similar name alone does not prove identical weights. The allenpoe repositories using “converted” in the name are not automatically classified as format conversions because their cards discuss transcript transformations.

Research coverage outside the Hub includes [Idiap's six-model CTC suite](https://github.com/idiap/w2v2-air-traffic), [WhisperATC](https://github.com/jlvdoorn/WhisperATC), and [ATCO2's project resources](https://www.atco2.org/data). The first two point to checkpoints already represented here. ATCO2 reports historical Kaldi results; this assessment has not verified a ready-to-run public Kaldi artifact for those particular results. A paper or training recipe alone does not become an executable candidate. Commercial systems and paper-only results are not mixed into the checkpoint inventory.

## Data lineage and release implications

Three distinctions matter:

1. **Known shared source:** our Jacktol audit establishes links to ATCO2 source recordings. It applies directly to the audited dataset revision, not automatically to every later remix. For Jacktol-adapted descendants, trace the exact inherited training history.
2. **Possible shared source:** ATCO2-derived training is a reason to audit recordings and segments. It is not proof that a model trained on our exact test utterances. tclin's ATCOSIM naming conflicts with its combined-dataset link; do not treat it as ATCOSIM-only without resolving that discrepancy.
3. **Different declared source:** US tower recordings, virtual-ATC YouTube, and synthetic-only adaptation are useful transfer candidates. They still require provenance checks before any assertion of complete independence.

The inventory's license field reproduces the publisher's metadata, not a verified legal conclusion. Repository licenses, pretrained-weight terms, training-corpus restrictions, teacher/synthetic-model terms, and redistribution permissions are different facts. MIT/Apache metadata does not remove a UWB restriction or establish that all upstream rights are covered. Several repositories explicitly declare noncommercial, research, “other,” or Llama-related terms. Keep benchmark suitability separate from eligibility for our NVIDIA release.

N4 remains the selected streaming release path; an offline model's lower lexical WER would not by itself satisfy that streaming objective.

## Reproducible evaluation protocol

1. **Freeze inputs and candidates.** Pin model revisions from the JSON snapshot, exact weight files and SHA-256 hashes after download, processor/base revisions for adapters, runtime/container versions, and decoding settings. For ASTRA, specify the subdirectory and actual ASR asset. A repository pin alone does not select one of several checkpoints.
2. **Verify the test identity.** Use the existing 1,908-record manifest and reference normalization. Its recorded manifest SHA-256 is `4af85f51d32051ee84c841c92c8e5e1eb69ee07201149197f905a7edc9c13082`. Confirm against the evaluation artifact before running. Materialize exact source-recording offsets and durations into utterance crops; never feed entire source recordings accidentally.
3. **Resolve overlap per candidate.** Match source metadata and audio fingerprints against accessible training material, including earlier fine-tuning stages. Reuse the known 1,695-record mask as a labeled supplementary view. If additional overlap requires another mask, score every compared model on that same mask and publish its counts. Do not compare scores with different denominators as a ranking.
4. **Smoke-test on development audio.** Check restoration, English transcription, silence behavior, long clips, precision, batch sizing, truncation, and output parsing on development material. Set decisions before the final test. In particular, inspect rasr's documented 18-second constraint, Whisper's long-input behavior, generation length caps, and raw Canary/ASTRA checkpoint restoration. Do not silently drop unsupported clips.
5. **Primary ASR condition.** Use audio-only transcription, fixed utterance boundaries, and a declared deterministic decoder. No reference-derived prompts, airport/callsign lists from test labels, external LLM correction, or ADS-B resolver. Preserve established N4/P4 decoding for their comparison. An author's optional n-gram LM, contextual biasing, denoising, or production pipeline gets a separately named condition rather than being silently mixed into the primary score.
6. **Score consistently.** Use `scripts/score_wer.py`: corpus substitutions + deletions + insertions divided by reference words, with the existing case/Unicode/symbol/whitespace normalization. Preserve raw text as well as normalized text. Do not import a model card's article removal or number folding into the primary scorer. Any secondary number-normalized metric must be fixed and applied to all systems equally.
7. **Report complete outcomes.** Save per-utterance hypotheses, error counts, reference word counts, all 1,908 row IDs, failures/truncations, wall time, audio duration, GPU/runtime, peak memory, and batch size. Distinguish failed/incomplete runs from scored results. Do not report success-only WER after dropping errors. Use paired, source-recording-clustered bootstrap intervals for meaningful comparisons; tiny differences may not be resolved by two hours of audio.
8. **Separate throughput from streaming.** Report offline real-time factor and N4 streaming latency under their actual operating modes. A fast offline decoder or sliding-window wrapper is not evidence of native cached streaming. Reviewed community cards have not established a directly equivalent streaming ATC baseline to N4.

Model-specific tokenization and decoding differ, so an identical numerical beam width is not a universal fairness requirement. The essential contract is identical audio/references, disclosed decoding, no test-driven tuning, complete output coverage, and consistent scoring.

## Execution order and expected deliverables

**First pass completed:** see the [benchmark report](community-atc-benchmark-2026-11-06.md). The evaluated group was qenneth (reuse the pinned result/predictions if their evaluation identity matches), Jacktol Whisper large v3, YouTube FastConformer, XLS-R ATCOSIM, STEAK synthetic Whisper, US Whisper Turbo v3, jlvdoorn Whisper large v3 ATCO2, and rasr Parakeet v1/v2. Include N4 and P4 as frozen baselines; compute the common supplementary view from stored predictions where available.

**Second pass:** the Tier B suite after restoration/provenance checks. This contains both expanded architectural coverage and controlled family ablations. **Third pass:** Tier C and the remaining documented longlist, plus conversion parity runs if deployment decisions require them. Repositories without weights or an identifiable speech task remain unresolved rather than receiving invented benchmark scores.

GPU-hours cannot be responsibly estimated from model-card WERs. Measure development-set runtime per model on the intended GPU, then extrapolate from actual audio duration and include load/decoding overhead. The initial group uses NeMo, Transformers/PEFT, and CTC paths; later groups add Qwen/Voxtral/SpeechLM and special preprocessing.

The resulting benchmark should publish a comparison table with checkpoint/revision, training-source class, overlap status, full-test WER, common-subset WER, confidence intervals, substitution/deletion/insertion counts, runtime, memory, and failure count. A second table should document optional LM/context/denoising conditions. Predictions and source audio stay in the existing restricted evaluation location; only appropriate aggregate results and reproducibility metadata belong in the public study repository.
